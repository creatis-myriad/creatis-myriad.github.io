# Highlights

* [SAM-Med3D (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.15161) introduces a general-purpose framework for volumetric medical image segmentation. It aims to accurately segment diverse anatomical structures across multiple imaging modalities using only a few 3D point prompts.
* The code is available on the [official Git repo](https://github.com/uni-medical/SAM-Med3D).

&nbsp;
# Introduction

Medical image analysis plays a central role in modern healthcare, supporting tasks such as disease diagnosis and treatment planning. Among these tasks, 3D segmentation is particularly important because volumetric data captures spatial and structural information that cannot be fully represented in 2D images.

Today, task-specific models (eg. nnU-Net [^1]) achieve high performance on predefined tasks. However, they generalize poorly to unseen structures or domains. New applications often require retraining dedicated models, leading to high development costs.

Therefore, there is a real need for a general-purpose segmentation model for volumetric medical imaging. In natural images, Segment Anything Model (SAM, 2023) [^2] has demonstrated impressive zero-shot ability to new image distributions and tasks. However, its 2D architecture cannot be directly applied to 3D medical data. Several approaches (SAM3D [^3], 3DSAM-Adapter [^4]) attempted to extend SAM to volumetric data while retaining its 2D image encoder, but achieved limited success.

To address these challenges, SAM-Med3D proposes a fully learnable 3D architecture trained on SA-Med3D-140K, a large-scale dataset composed of public and licensed private medical data. The model offers three main advantages: 
- **improved interaction efficiency** through sparse 3D point prompts,
- **universal segmentation capabilities** enabled by large-scale and diverse training data, offering satisfying segmentations across anatomical structures and imaging modalities,
- **strong transferability**, demonstrating promising performance on previously unseen target tasks and serving as an effective pretrained foundation model.

&nbsp;

# SA-Med3D-140K Dataset 
SA-Med3D-140K is one of the largest volumetric medical segmentation datasets available at the time of publication. It combines data from 70 public datasets and 24 private datasets, resulting in 22k 3D medical images and 143k segmentation masks.

The dataset covers 28 imaging modalities, including CT, ultrasound, and 26 MRI sequences, spanning six major anatomical regions. It is split into 131k training masks and 12k validation masks. The training set contains 58 public datasets and all private data, while the validation set consists of 16 public benchmark datasets, some of which have their training set included the training sources.

# Method

## SAM-Med3D Archiecture 
<div style="text-align:center">
<img src="/collections/images/SAMMed3D/sammed3darchi.jpg" width=1500></div>
<p style="text-align: center;font-style:italic">Figure 1. The fully 3D architecture of our SAM-Med3D.</p>

SAM-Med3D is trained from scratch on volumetric medical data using a fully 3D architecture. Unlike previous approaches that adapt SAM's 2D encoder, the model directly processes 3D images and 3D prompts through dedicated 3D components, including positional encodings, convolutions, and normalization layers.

This design better captures volumetric spatial information and reduces the number of prompt points required for accurate segmentation.


&nbsp;

## Training in 2 stages
Inspired by the pretraining–finetuning paradigm used in large language models, SAM-Med3D is trained in two stages.

- **Stage 1: Pre-training** consists of large-scale pretraining on the complete training set until convergence.
- **Stage 2: Fine-tuning** on a curated subset of 75k high-quality masks for the training set.

SAM-Med3D consistently outperforms both SAM and SAM-Med2D across different numbers of prompt points while requiring fewer user interactions.

The fully 3D architecture also improves efficiency. Depending on the target size, inference time ranges from only 1% to 26% of that required by SAM. When the target spans more than 20 slices, corresponding roughly to objects larger than 3 cm, SAM-Med3D becomes the more efficient solution.

## Comparison with other state of the art specific and generalist models


The authors compare SAM-Med3D with both task-specific and general-purpose segmentation methods on six public benchmarks. Task-specific baselines include the CNN-based nnU-Net and the Transformer-based UNETR, each trained separately on the corresponding benchmark dataset.

<div style="text-align:center">

<p style="text-align: center;font-style:italic"> Table 3. Comparison of task-specific and general-purpose methods on 6 public benchmarks (task-specific models are trained on the training set of each dataset). Results show that nnU-Net achieves higher accuracy than SAM-Med3D when only one click is provided. With ten clicks, the performance gap becomes small, and nnU-Net still outperforms SAM-Med3D on some datasets.  

This highlights the trade-off between a universal interactive model and specialized models optimized for a single dataset : SAM-Med3D relies on a single pre-trained model applicable across datasets, whereas nnU-Net requires task-specific training but achieves higher segmentation accuracy.
# Conclusion

SAM-Med3D is one of the first fully learnable 3D adaptations of the SAM paradigm. Its main contribution is the replacement of the 2D architecture with a native 3D design, enabling efficient interaction through sparse 3D prompts and improved volumetric consistency.

Compared with SAM and SAM-Med2D, it achieves better accuracy with fewer interactions and substantially lower inference time. 
However, task-specific models such as nnU-Net remain competitive and often achieve superior performance, particularly when only a limited number of interaction points are provided.
The results suggest that SAM-Med3D is most valuable as a general-purpose interactive segmentation model rather than a replacement for highly optimized task-specific approaches.
