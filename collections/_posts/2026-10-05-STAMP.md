# Highlights

* **Joint subtyping and anatomical forecasting:** STAMP learns a mixture of continuous-time progression trajectories together with dense 3D MRI deformations, rather than clustering subjects after fitting a shared trajectory.
* **Interpretable personalization:** baseline regional volumes and clinical scores determine subtype weights, while a subject-specific time shift places each patient along a shared disease timeline.
* **Stable subtype learning:** time-binned balancing, entropy regularization, and curriculum scheduling encourage distinct progression regimes without simply separating early from late disease stages.
* **ADNI evaluation:** the authors report the lowest regional volume forecasting error in 16 of 18 regions, including ties, and significant improvements over CounterSynth in 11 regions.
* [Code repository announced by the authors](https://gitlab.inria.fr/jkalkhof/stamp) and [MICCAI paper page](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/0998-Paper3598.html).

# Introduction

Alzheimer's disease does not progress identically across patients. A useful progression model should describe both **when a patient is along a disease trajectory** and **which pattern of progression best explains their anatomy**.

Existing approaches address different parts of this problem:

* **Biomarker-based subtyping**, such as SuStaIn[^1], estimates subtypes and disease stages using scalar measurements. This supports patient stratification but does not directly learn fine-grained anatomical changes from full images.
* **Image-based longitudinal models**, such as spatio-temporal atlases and generative MRI forecasting methods, capture spatial changes. However, flexible individual variation does not necessarily produce explicit, interpretable progression subtypes.

STAMP combines these ideas: subtype-specific regional dynamics condition a deformation model that forecasts a patient's future brain anatomy. Its atlas backbone builds on SMART[^2], extending continuous-time image progression modeling with a learned gate between multiple trajectories.

# Methods

## Overview

The model is trained on longitudinal T1-weighted MRI series. For a new subject, it uses baseline anatomy, its segmentation, baseline clinical information, and a requested follow-up interval to predict anatomical evolution.

There are three levels of modeling:

1. **Temporal alignment:** locate the subject on a shared disease time axis.
2. **Regional progression:** integrate the dynamics of each subtype over the requested interval and combine them using subject-specific weights.
3. **Voxel-level deformation:** project the regional signal into image space and predict a deformation of the baseline MRI.

All components are optimized jointly. Although the method operates on 3D images, its temporal alignment and subtype gate also depend on regional summaries and clinical inputs.

## Continuous-time regional trajectories

For each subtype $$s \in \{1,\ldots,C\}$$, STAMP learns a progression function

$$
g^s(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{K},
$$

where $$K$$ is the number of brain regions and $$g_k^s(t)$$ is the instantaneous regional progression signal at disease time $$t$$. The functions are parameterized using neural ordinary differential equations (neural ODEs)[^3], allowing nonlinear temporal dynamics.

A time-shift estimator uses baseline relative regional volumes to estimate the subject-specific position $$v^j$$ on the disease axis. The same subject position is used when evaluating the different subtype trajectories. For a forecasting interval $$\delta t$$, the integrated signal for subject $$j$$ and subtype $$s$$ is

$$
I^{j,s}(\delta t;v^j)
= \int_{v^j}^{v^j+\delta t} g^s(t)\,dt.
$$

Thus, disease time is distinct from chronological age: subjects with different ages can occupy similar positions on the learned progression axis. Personalization combines this temporal position with subtype membership and baseline anatomy; the paper describes a time shift rather than a separate subject-specific progression-speed parameter.

## Subtype gating

A gating network receives baseline relative regional volumes and clinical information. It outputs a probability-like vector

$$
\theta^j \in \Delta^{C-1}, \qquad
\theta_s^j \geq 0, \qquad \sum_{s=1}^{C}\theta_s^j=1.
$$

The regional signal passed to the deformation model is the convex combination

$$
I^j = \sum_{s=1}^{C}\theta_s^j I^{j,s}.
$$

The reported experiments learn **two subtypes**. Although the assignments are encouraged to become confident, the mathematical formulation uses a weighted mixture rather than requiring a hard subtype selection.

## From regional signals to MRI deformations

The baseline segmentation projects each regional signal onto the corresponding voxels, creating a dense growth map. This conditions a deformation backbone based on **M3D-NCA**, a 3D neural cellular automaton[^4]. Local, recurrent voxel updates predict a stationary velocity field, which is integrated to obtain a diffeomorphic displacement used to warp the baseline image.


The growth signal is a non-negative, region-level conditioning variable for the learned deformation model, rather than a directly reported signed volume-change measurement. The deformation model uses this signal, together with baseline anatomy, to predict a voxel-level deformation field; therefore the sign and anatomical interpretation of the resulting local volume change are not specified directly by the value of the growth signal.

## Preventing subtype collapse

Jointly learning trajectories and assignments can produce degenerate solutions: all subjects may use one subtype, assignments may remain diffuse, or the gate may merely divide subjects by disease stage.

**Entropy regularization** encourages confident assignments. For a minibatch of $$M$$ subjects,

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ent}}
= \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}H(\theta^j),
\qquad
H(\theta^j)=-\sum_{s=1}^{C}\theta_s^j\log\theta_s^j.
$$

Minimizing this loss sharpens the gate, but does not by itself prevent every subject from choosing the same subtype.

**Time-binned balancing** constrains subtype usage within bins of the estimated disease axis. If $$S_b$$ contains subjects in bin $$b$$, average subtype usage is

$$
u_{b,s}=\frac{1}{|S_b|}\sum_{j\in S_b}\theta_s^j.
$$

A smooth barrier penalizes values outside $$[\alpha,1-\alpha]$$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bin}}
=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{s=1}^{C}\phi(u_{b,s}),
$$

where $$B$$ is the number of time bins. An exponential moving average reduces noise in the estimated usage. Requiring multiple subtypes at each stage discourages a split that simply assigns early and late disease to different clusters.

**Curriculum scheduling** emphasizes balancing early, when trajectories are not yet reliable, then reduces its weight and increases entropy regularization to consolidate specialization. The training objective combines the forecasting loss with these two regularizers. The paper does not specify the exact forecasting-loss formula or numerical schedule.

# Experiments

## Data and setup

* **Cohort:** ADNI-1/GO/2, with 446 subjects and 1,041 scans; at least two time points per subject and a mean follow-up of 2.95 years.
* **Selection:** available cognitive assessments (ADAS11, FAQ, MMSE, CDRSB, MOCA) and confirmed amyloid pathology, using $$A\beta < 192\ \mathrm{pg/mL}$$.
* **Preprocessing with turboprep[^5]:** MRI scans are skull-stripped and affinely aligned, then resampled to $$128 \times 160 \times 128$$ voxels. Brain tissue segmentation is obtained with SynthSeg 2.0.
* **Split:** 80% training, 5% validation, and 15% testing, separated at the patient level.
* **Training:** PyTorch and Adam.
* **Comparisons:** CounterSynth, [BrLP]({% post_url 2026-03-05-Brain-latent-progression %}), and Conditional VoxelMorph, reproduced from official implementations with matched preprocessing, resolution, and splits.

## Anatomical forecasting

Table 1 compares regional volume forecasting across all 18 regions. The mean absolute error (MAE) is expressed as a percentage of total brain volume and reported as mean ± standard deviation. Lower values are better; bold entries mark the best results, and asterisks indicate significant pairwise differences ($$p<0.05$$).

The authors report the lowest MAE in **16 of 18 regions**, with some ties at the displayed precision. CounterSynth performs better in cerebellar white matter and the caudate. STAMP is significantly better than CounterSynth in **11 regions**, using two-tailed paired t-tests with $$p<0.05$$.

Geometric agreement, averaged across regions, is:

| Method | Dice (higher is better) | Hausdorff95 (lower is better) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STAMP | 0.907 | 1.010 |
| CounterSynth | 0.907 | 1.018 |
| Conditional VoxelMorph | 0.856 | 1.065 |
| BrLP | 0.665 | 2.207 |

The main advantage over CounterSynth is therefore in regional volumetric forecasting; their average geometric scores are very similar. These comparisons concern the reproduced models on this cohort and split.

## Learned progression regimes

<figure style="text-align:center;">
  <img src="/collections/images/stamp/stamp-regional-progressions.jpg" width="700" alt="Subtype-specific regional growth signals over reparameterized disease time, with blue and orange trajectories">
  <figcaption><i>Figure 3 from the paper. The two subtypes have distinct regional progression signals along the shared disease axis.</i></figcaption>
</figure>

The learned functions show different progression dynamics across regions. The paper reports associations between the subtype gate and clinical or biomarker variables, including ADAS13 ($$p<10^{-13}$$), amyloid-β ($$p<10^{-6}$$), TAU ($$p<10^{-6}$$), and APOE4 carrier frequency ($$p<10^{-2}$$).

* 95% of cognitively normal subjects are assigned to Subtype 1.
* 100% of dementia cases are assigned to Subtype 2.
* Subjects with mild cognitive impairment span both trajectories.

The estimated time shift correlates moderately with ADAS13 (Spearman $$\rho=0.43$$, $$p<10^{-3}$$). Subtype assignment is not significantly correlated with time shift ($$p>0.05$$), and both subtypes cover the estimated disease axis. This supports a distinction between stage and progression regime, although a nonsignificant correlation does not establish independence.

The authors interpret these groups as **data-driven progression regimes**, rather than definitive biological disease subtypes.

Removing subtypes increases average error from 0.092 to 0.107. Removing any of the stabilization components also degrades the overall result, with the largest average degradation occurring without curriculum scheduling.

Inverting the gate gives a smaller average degradation (0.092 to 0.094). Significant differences are marked for the lateral and inferior lateral ventricles in this comparison. This supports the usefulness of personalized routing, while showing that its effect varies by region.

# Conclusions

* STAMP connects explicit progression subtyping to continuous-time, deformation-based MRI forecasting within one jointly trained model.
* Regional dynamics and the shared disease axis provide an interpretable intermediate representation between patient-level gating and voxel-level anatomical change.
* The ADNI results support improved regional volume prediction, and the ablations show that learning useful subtypes requires careful regularization and scheduling.

# Remarks

* **External validation:** evaluation is restricted to an amyloid-positive ADNI cohort. Generalization to independent cohorts, other populations, and different acquisition settings remains to be established.
* **Meaning and number of subtypes:** the experiments report two progression regimes. The paper does not provide a study selecting the number of subtypes or demonstrate that these groups are stable biological categories. Their strong diagnostic associations also warrant further study.
* **Statistical interpretation:** the reported regional tests use $$p<0.05$$; the paper does not describe correction for multiple comparisons across regions.
* **Reproducibility:** the main paper does not specify the numerical solver used for ODE integration. It also does not provide numerical details for the bin bounds, scheduling weights, or several training hyperparameters. The announced implementation is relevant for reproducing these choices.
* **Clinical scope:** anatomical forecasting and biomarker associations are evaluated, but treatment-response prediction and prospective clinical utility are not demonstrated.
